## 1. Verdict

The documentation system is better than the project it documents.

The `docs` repo is unusually disciplined: separation of living state from frozen decisions, invariants as explicit constraints, an automation-readiness test, a hygiene check in CI, a dependency-graph work queue. The engineering-of-knowledge layer is sound and should be preserved.

Three foundational gaps sit underneath it:

1. **The value chain models only the fulfilment half of the business.** Supplier feed to delivered order is specified in 19 stages. Acquisition — content production, translation, SEO asset generation, merchandising — appears in no VC stage, so the north-star metric (SC-07, "automation level per stage L2+") does not cover the operator's largest recurring workload, in a business whose declared primary acquisition channel is organic search. See AUD-01.
2. **The commercial, legal and financial substrate has no home in the corpus at all.** Zero occurrences of GDPR, privacy, personal data, legal entity, invoice, KYC. One passing mention of VAT. No unit-economics model, no PSP decision, no answer to where the operator is legally established — which determines VAT treatment, EPR obligations and whether REG-06's Authorised Representative requirement bites. The routing table has no row for any of it. See AUD-03.
3. **The plan front-loads specification and back-loads evidence.** Five load-bearing facts are self-declared unverified, including the capacity bet behind INV-01 and the supplier feed nobody has seen. Phase -1's exit gate is a 19-stage paper checklist with no instrument. See AUD-07.

The strongest single artefact is ADR-0006. It reasons explicitly about reversibility, designs the degenerate single-supplier case, and states its accepted costs. It is the standard the rest of the decision record should be held to — and the ADR template does not ask for any of what makes it good (AUD-18).

The most consequential unexamined premise is not technical. It is that 10-20 storefronts, built from the same distributor feeds, with copy generated from structured attributes, can win organic search in Dutch. Every architectural optimisation in the repo — edge hosting, Core Web Vitals budgets, the whole INV-08 priority order — is downstream of that premise being true, and it has never been tested or written down as a risk. See AUD-02.

## 2. Findings — S1, foundational

### AUD-01 — The value chain omits the acquisition half of the business
`JUDGEMENT`

- `00-product/automation-charter.md` defines VC-01..VC-19: supplier onboarding, feed, normalisation, enrichment, assortment, pricing, freshness, publication, storefront onboarding, order capture, sourcing, PO, acknowledgement, tracking, customer comms, payment, returns, reconciliation, monitoring.
- Every stage is fulfilment-side. Nothing covers: product copy generation, translation to the market language, legal-text localisation, SEO asset production (titles, meta, category copy, internal linking), merchandising and category strategy, or channel listing if marketplaces are ever adopted.
- `00-product/vision.md:24` makes organic search a primary acquisition channel and a revenue variable. `00-product/market-selection.md:20` (MC-05) makes attribute-generated content a binding constraint on vertical selection. Both point at work that the charter does not model.
- Consequence: SC-07 targets L2+ automation per stage and therefore certifies nothing about the work the operator will actually spend most weeks doing. A charter that is declared the north star, and is read before every PRD, tech spec and ADR review, has a blind spot the size of the revenue engine.
- Consequence for the automation-readiness test: content production is exactly the kind of process that decays into "the operator does it by hand each time", which is anti-pattern #1 in the charter's own list.

Remedy:
- Add VC stages for content generation, localisation/translation, SEO asset production and merchandising, with `Now`/`Target` levels like every other stage. Numbering: append VC-20+ rather than renumbering — VC ids are referenced elsewhere.
- Or state explicitly in the charter that acquisition is out of its scope and name the doc that owns it. Either is defensible; silence is not.
- Legal-text localisation is a special case: `market-selection.md:51` already identifies LLM-translated German legal text as direct Abmahnung exposure. The same reasoning applies at lower intensity to Dutch consumer-law texts. Do not let it enter as an ordinary translation step.

### AUD-02 — The acquisition thesis is the largest unexamined premise in the project
`JUDGEMENT`

- The business model is: 10-20 niche storefronts, catalogs sourced from EU distributor feeds, copy generated from structured attributes, organic search as primary acquisition.
- Every element of that is stated. Their combination is never examined.
- The specific untested risk: distributor feeds give every reseller the same products, same EANs, and frequently the same manufacturer descriptions. Attribute-generated copy on top of a syndicated catalog is thin-content-shaped by construction. Running that pattern across 10-20 own domains with acknowledged catalog overlap (`30-frontend/README.md:39` explicitly anticipates overlapping catalogs across tenants) sits close to the doorway-site pattern search engines penalise.
- No ADR addresses acquisition strategy. No entry in `vision.md`'s structural-risk list covers it — that list is entirely about supply-side failure modes (stock, price, lead time, feed drift, supplier concentration). The demand side has no named risk at all.
- `docs-pjl` (keyword competition analysis) is scoped to search volume and top-10 domain strength. That answers "can a page rank here" and not "can *this content model* rank here", which is the question the business depends on.
- bol.com appears once (`market-selection.md:87`), as a competitive threat. Marketplace gravity is scored as a market-selection negative. Selling *through* bol.com — the standard low-CAC entry for a Dutch dropshipper, and a channel that does not care about content differentiation — is never evaluated as an option.
- Consequence if the premise is false: INV-08's priority order is wrong. Cost > CWV/SEO > maintenance > time-to-launch only makes sense if SEO is the channel. If acquisition turns out to be paid or marketplace-led, ADR-0001 (build a custom storefront for CWV control) and ADR-0003 (edge hosting to protect margin at organic scale) are both optimising the wrong variable, and the cheapest correct answer might have been Hyva on native multistore — which ADR-0001 itself names as the runner-up.

Remedy:
- Write an ADR for acquisition channel strategy. It is a one-way door in practice: it determines what the storefront platform is for.
- Extend `docs-pjl`'s scope: it must answer whether attribute-generated content can differentiate against incumbents holding the head terms, not only whether the terms are winnable in principle.
- Add demand-side risks to `vision.md`'s structural-risk list. It currently reads as if only supply can fail.
- Evaluate marketplace-as-channel explicitly, even if to reject it. Rejecting it in writing is a decision; not considering it is a gap.

Specific holes, each with a downstream consequence already implied elsewhere in the corpus:

- **Legal establishment of the operator is unstated.** `market-selection.md:31` (REG-06) says a seller *not established* in a member state must appoint an EPR Authorised Representative there from 2026-08-12 — thirteen days from this audit's date if NL is entered as a non-established seller. Whether that applies is unknown because the corpus never says where the operator is established. The same fact decides VAT registration and OSS treatment, EPR registration route, and consumer-law jurisdiction. It is a one-way door and it gates ADR-0007's own execution.
- **No unit economics.** SC-08 ("stable long-term profitability") is a `TODO(human)`. There is no margin model, no CAC assumption, no break-even, no per-order cost stack. `vision.md:20` states "Revenue = margin between supplier cost and storefront price, minus acquisition and payment cost" and that line is the entire financial model. ADR-0007 accepts a hard negative — "gross margin is capped by EU distributor cost, and the business case must close without it" — against a business case that does not exist in written form.
- **No PSP decision.** `market-selection.md:63` states iDEAL is a launch requirement, not an option. There is no ADR, no container row in `c4-container.md`, no owner for VC-16, and no entry in the c4-context beyond "Payment providers - OPEN". Payment is a launch blocker with no artefact.
- **No GDPR posture.** Every order transmits customer name and delivery address to a supplier. That is a controller-to-processor-or-controller transfer on the critical path of the system's core flow. No DPA requirement in the supplier onboarding gate (VC-01), no privacy policy, no records of processing, no retention rule, no mention in the supplier feed or PO contract specifications. ADR-0007 built a rigorous compliance gate for product safety and none for data protection.
- **No B2B/VAT-ID handling.** The recommended vertical is hand tools and workshop consumables — a segment with a substantial business-buyer tail. `market-selection.md:76` notes B2B payment-term expectations as a negative for the Horeca alternate but not for the primary candidate. Business customers imply VAT ID validation, reverse charge, invoice requirements, and different returns rights. None is modelled.
- **No consumer-law text plan for NL.** Withdrawal policy, terms, unit pricing. `market-selection.md:33-34` researched this in depth for Germany and did not carry the equivalent question to the chosen market.

Remedy:
- Two new routed docs: `00-product/commercial-model.md` (unit economics, margin stack, shipping cost, payment cost, break-even, target AOV) and `00-product/legal-and-operations.md` (establishment, VAT/OSS, EPR route, GDPR posture, consumer-law texts, PSP requirements). Add routing rows.
- One ADR for legal establishment. It gates too many downstream obligations to remain implicit.
- Add a DPA/data-processing gate to VC-01 alongside the existing GPSR evidence gate, and carry customer-data fields explicitly in the PO contract when it is written.
- Fold "does the business case close at EU distributor margin" into the Phase -1 exit gate. ADR-0007 accepted it as a cost; nothing tests it.

### AUD-04 — Sourcing is modelled per order line; the economically decisive decision is per order
`JUDGEMENT`, with `VERIFIED` supporting absences

- `10-architecture/domain-model.md:25` defines `SourcingDecision` as "which offer fulfils which order line". `glossary.md:22` repeats it per-line.
- `domain-model.md:42` lists the sourcing strategy options: cheapest offer, fastest offer, supplier priority. All three are per-line optimisations. Basket consolidation — minimising the number of POs per order — is absent from the option list.
- "shipping cost", "freight", "carriage" occur zero times in the corpus. The supplier feed contract sketch (`api-contracts/README.md:14`) lists required fields as "cost, units, currency, lead time" and does not name shipping tariff, free-shipping threshold, or per-parcel handling fee.
- `vision.md:45` names split shipments as a structural risk, framed operationally: "multiple tracking numbers, partial fulfilment". The financial consequence is not stated.
- The financial consequence is the decisive one. In dropshipping, the customer pays one shipping fee and the operator pays one per supplier parcel. Splitting a three-line basket across two suppliers to save a few percent on line cost routinely destroys more margin than the line saving recovers. A per-line cheapest-offer rule will do this systematically and invisibly.
- Consequence: the current model structurally forecloses the correct optimisation. Making sourcing order-scoped later is not a rule change — it changes what `SourcingDecision` *is*, and it is persisted per line from day one (ADR-0006, Process section, which mandates persisting a `SourcingDecision` per order line even with a single candidate).

Remedy:
- Redefine `SourcingDecision` as order-scoped, producing per-line assignments. The persisted per-line record stays; its parent becomes the decision unit.
- Add to the supplier feed contract: shipping tariff model, free-shipping threshold, per-parcel handling fee, and whether the supplier consolidates multi-line POs into one parcel.
- Add basket consolidation to the OPEN option list in `domain-model.md:42`, and state the objective function explicitly — the rule is not "cheapest offer" but "highest contribution margin per order subject to lead-time promise".
- This is cheap now and expensive after the first PO contract is written. Prioritise it above other domain-model work.

### AUD-05 — The binding constraint is declared and never modelled against the plan
`JUDGEMENT`

- `vision.md:37` — "Single operator. No human team. Capacity is the binding constraint on every plan."
- What the plan commits to building before storefront #1 earns anything: a custom Next.js multi-tenant storefront platform with its own design system, a catalog data layer, feed ingestion, per-channel supplier adapters, order routing and PO lifecycle, reconciliation, freshness monitoring, and an exception surface (AUD-06). ADR-0001 accepts explicitly: "two runtimes to operate solo; all commerce UI must be built rather than inherited". ADR-0005's dedicated-service option adds a third.
- What the plan commits to operating: Magento 2 + PHP-FPM, Node on Cloudflare Workers, MySQL/MariaDB, OpenSearch, Valkey/Redis, Varnish, RabbitMQ, imgproxy, plus CI and visual regression. Nine runtime components, solo, to L3 automation on nine of nineteen value-chain stages.
- `roadmap.md:12` cites Shape Up — "4-6 week cycles, Kanban with WIP = 1". Shape Up's central mechanism is the *appetite*: fixed time, variable scope. No appetite is stated anywhere in the corpus, for any phase or any artefact. The one mechanism that would bind scope to capacity is named and not used.
- The roadmap's exit gates are quality gates only. No gate anywhere is an effort or elapsed-time gate. Phase -1 is explicitly unbounded (`vision.md:38`).
- ADR-0001's revisit trigger — "solo operation of two runtimes proves unsustainable in Phase 1" — has no metric, no threshold and no observer. It will trigger, if it triggers, as burnout rather than as a measurement.

This is not an argument to reduce ambition. It is an argument that the corpus asserts a constraint it never applies, which means the constraint cannot arbitrate anything.

Remedy:
- Add `Appetite` to the ADR and PRD templates: the time the operator is willing to spend before the decision is revisited, not the estimate of the work.
- Add an operating-load budget to `vision.md` under Constraints: maximum number of independently-failing runtime components, maximum number of alert sources, and a weekly ops-hours ceiling. Then test ADR-0003's and ADR-0005's options against it. It is the missing tiebreaker that INV-08's priority order does not supply, because "maintenance effort" is ranked but never quantified.
- Rewrite unmeasurable revisit triggers (AUD-21).

### AUD-06 — The exception path has no owner, no container and no contract
`VERIFIED`

- The automation-readiness test's question 6 requires every design to define an escalation path carrying enough context to act on. The charter's north star: "Human attention is an exception path, not a step."
- `c4-context.md:15` has an `ADMIN[Admin surfaces]` node. It is the only mention. `c4-container.md` has no row for it — no runtime, no repo, no owner. No entity in `domain-model.md` represents an exception, a failed item, or a work queue for the operator. No state machine covers it. No contract in `api-contracts/README.md` produces it. `40-devops/README.md:28-29` gives monitoring four lines and says alerts link to runbooks; there is one runbook and it is a draft.
- The failure modes the docs already name and route to a human: unmatched products (VC-03), incomplete content (VC-04), feed quarantine (VC-02), sourcing failure, PO rejection, backorder, partial fulfilment (SM-order, SM-purchase-order branches), stale offers (SM-offer), reconciliation drift (VC-18). Every one of those needs a surface. None has one.
- Consequence: the single component the solo operator lives in daily is the least specified part of the architecture, and it is specified nowhere at all rather than specified badly.
- Second consequence: because the exception surface is undefined, question 6 of the readiness test cannot actually be answered by any future design. The test will be answered with prose ("escalates to the operator") that means nothing, and the charter's central instrument becomes ceremonial.

Remedy:
- Add an `Operations console` row to `c4-container.md` with an explicit TBD owner, and an ADR for its shape: Magento admin extension, a purpose-built app, or notification-channel-plus-runbook. This interacts with ADR-0005 — if the catalog data layer is a dedicated service, its exceptions cannot surface in Magento admin without a second integration.
- Model the exception as a first-class entity in `domain-model.md` with a state machine (`raised -> acknowledged -> resolved | escalated | auto_resolved`) and a required context payload.
- Make question 6 of the readiness test cite the surface, not describe it.

### AUD-11 — The hygiene gate is materially weaker than the conventions it enforces
`VERIFIED` — reproduced empirically on a copy of the repo

Test performed: copied `docs/` to a scratch directory, planted two files, ran `python3 scripts/docs-check.py`.

| Planted defect | Detected |
|---|---|
| `60-decisions/ADR-9999-orphan-test.md`, absent from the routing table | **no** |
| `50-runbooks/orphan-runbook.md`, absent from the routing table | **no** |
| broken path in `related:` frontmatter | **no** |
| broken markdown-style link `[x](../00-product/also-missing.md)` | **no** |
| broken backticked reference `` `10-architecture/nonexistent-file.md` `` | yes |

Root causes, in `scripts/docs-check.py`:

- Line 67: `if rel not in index_text and f"{directory}/" not in index_text`. Because `60-decisions/`, `50-runbooks/`, `70-tenants/` and `90-archive/` all appear as bare directory strings in the routing table, **no file inside any of them can ever be reported as an orphan**. The orphan check — mechanism #2 of the three that `CONVENTIONS.md:50` says prevent rot — is effectively inert for four of eight directories, including the one that holds every decision.
- Line 83: the link regex requires backticks. Markdown links and `related:` frontmatter paths are never checked, so the frontmatter relation graph can rot freely.

Conventions declared in `CONVENTIONS.md` and not implemented at all:
- duplicate-fact detection (`:66`) — the rule most violated in practice (AUD-12).
- marker age (`:65`, "a marker older than two phases is a decision being avoided") — markers are counted, never aged. 41 markers are reported with no way to tell a new one from an original-skeleton one.
- phase-based staleness (`:53`) — implemented as 90 calendar days (`STALE_DAYS = 90`), while Phase -1 is unbounded. The check and the rule measure different things, and nothing in the repo records phase boundaries in machine-readable form.

Remedy, in priority order:
1. Fix the orphan check: require an explicit path match, or require each directory-routed area to carry its own index that lists its files.
2. Extend link checking to markdown links and to `related:` frontmatter paths.
3. Add ID referential integrity (AUD-10).
4. Add duplicate-fact detection — even a crude normalised-sentence hash across files would have caught the AUD-12 duplications.
5. Age markers via `git log -S` or a `since:` field, and fail on markers older than a declared threshold.
6. Record phase boundaries somewhere machine-readable so "one phase" stops being approximated by 90 days.

Note on the CI gate: `.github/workflows/docs-hygiene.yml` runs on push and PR, but all 8 commits went directly to `main` and no PR has ever existed. `.github/pull_request_template.md` and the "Definition of Done" checklist are currently decoration. That is acceptable for a solo repo — but the corpus should say which gates are real and which are aspirational, otherwise a future session will trust a gate that never ran.
